# Getting the enquiry form live

A short guide for Patrycja. You set up the account and send Liam the key, and he
wires it in. About ten minutes of your time, none of it technical, and it costs
nothing.

---

## What this is, in one paragraph

The website has an enquiry form on the Contact page. It asks the questions you
would otherwise ask by email: what the document is, which country it is going
to, whether it needs an apostille, when you need it by. The idea is that a first
enquiry arrives with enough in it to quote a fixed fee, instead of starting a
back-and-forth.

A website cannot send email by itself, so the form uses a service called
**Web3Forms** to do the sending. It sits between the website and your inbox. The
account is yours, so the enquiries are always yours.

**Right now the form is deliberately switched off.** It will not pretend to
send. If someone tries, it tells them to call you instead, so no enquiry is ever
lost quietly. It stays that way until your key is in.

---

## Step 1: create the account

1. Go to **web3forms.com** and create an account with the email address you want
   enquiries delivered to. The site shows `office@sikorskanotary.co.uk`
   throughout, so ideally that one
2. The **free plan is all you need**. It covers 250 enquiries a month, which is
   comfortably more than the practice receives

There is no document upload on the form, as agreed, so there is nothing to pay
for. If you ever change your mind about attachments, that is the one thing that
needs the paid plan, and we can revisit it then.

---

## Step 2: send Liam the access key

In your Web3Forms account you will find an **access key**. It looks like
`a1b2c3d4-e5f6-7890-abcd-ef1234567890`.

**Email it to Liam.** That is your whole step. He embeds it into the site and
confirms back to you once a test enquiry has actually landed in your inbox.

The key is not a password. It only tells the service which inbox to deliver to,
but treat it like practice information rather than posting it anywhere public.

---

## Step 3: turn on the automatic reply

*Five minutes, in your Web3Forms dashboard, and the part most worth doing.*

The old form gave people nothing back. Someone with a deadline pressed Send and
had no idea whether it had worked. In the dashboard, find your form and turn on
**Auto Respond**. Wording you can paste in, adjusting anything that does not
sound like you:

> **Subject:** We have your enquiry, Sikorska Notary
>
> Thank you for contacting Sikorska Notary.
>
> We have your enquiry and will come back to you with the next steps, and a
> fixed fee where we can quote one.
>
> If your matter is urgent, please call 07401 388 094 rather than waiting on
> email.
>
> Sikorska Notary Limited
> Notarial services for England and Wales
> 07401 388 094 · office@sikorskanotary.co.uk

**Worth considering: a Polish line at the bottom.** Poland is the single largest
destination for documents leaving your practice, and some enquiries will come
through the Polish page. A draft to correct in your own words:

> Dziękujemy za kontakt z Sikorska Notary. Otrzymaliśmy Państwa zapytanie i
> wrócimy z kolejnymi krokami oraz wyceną. W sprawach pilnych prosimy o telefon:
> 07401 388 094.

Please check that Polish yourself before it goes out. It should be your words,
not ours.

---

## Step 4: test it, once Liam confirms the key is in

Send yourself two enquiries **from your phone**, not from a computer:

- one as **an individual**, one as **a business**
- check the email arrives
- check the **automatic reply** arrives
- check that pressing **Reply** goes back to the enquirer, not to yourself
- check the subject line reads sensibly. It is built for sorting an inbox, so it
  will look like `Business enquiry: Corporate power of attorney (Poland)`, and
  anything due within three days is prefixed `[URGENT]`

If any of that is wrong, better to find out now than from a client.

---

## About documents

The form does not accept attachments, which is what you asked for. Clients are
told on the Contact page to email the document to
`office@sikorskanotary.co.uk` after sending the enquiry, or bring it to the
appointment.

That also keeps your Privacy Notice simple. Nothing on the website receives or
stores client documents, so there is no third-party processor to describe.

---

## What to expect once it is on

- **Spam:** there is a hidden trap in the form that catches automated
  submissions. You should see very little
- **Where enquiries live:** in your inbox, and in the Web3Forms dashboard for 30
  days on the free plan. Treat your inbox as the real record
- **If it ever stops:** the form tells the visitor to call you rather than
  failing silently, so the worst case is a phone call instead of an email
